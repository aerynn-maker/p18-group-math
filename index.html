🌱 The Bioscience Problem

In Malaysia, oil palm sustainability is central to national food security and economic stability. As oil palm trees (*Elaeis guineensis*) mature, their Fresh Fruit Bunch (FFB) yield changes non-linearly.

While a standard biological model follows a biological growth curve, we can accurately model the highly productive phase (Years 3 to 20) using a cubic function. Managing replanting cycles requires understanding not just total yield, but the exact rate at which yield is increasing or decreasing at any given year. This is where the first derivative comes in.

Data Context: Based on historical Malaysian palm oil yield profiles documented by agencies like the Malaysian Palm Oil Board (MPOB) in their annual "Malaysian Palm Oil Statistics" report.

📐 Mathematical Modeling

Yield Function $f(x)$
$$f(x) = -0.015x^3 + 0.42x^2 - 1.8x + 5$$

Predicts annual FFB yield ($t/ha/yr$) where $x$ is tree age in years ($3 \le x \le 20$).

Derivative Function $f'(x)$
$$f'(x) = -0.045x^2 + 0.84x - 1.8$$

Calculated using the power rule. Represents the marginal yield rate ($t/ha/yr^2$) at exactly year $x$.
